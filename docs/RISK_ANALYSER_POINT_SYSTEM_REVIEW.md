# Review of the Previous Risk Analyser Point System

## Executive conclusion

The previous Risk Analyser's arithmetic was correct in the anonymised worked example. The selected answers add to `60`, and `60` is classified as **Moderately Aggressive** under the bands printed in that document.

The weakness was not the addition. It was the design, interpretation and governance of the methodology. The point system contained overlapping bands, disproportionate factor weighting, no explicitly scored investment objective, and insufficient protection against a client's stated tolerance obscuring limited financial capacity. The expected-income question itself was valid, but it was incorrectly completed in the worked example.

KCAS now offers this point system as a separately selectable methodology while retaining the evidenced KCAS methodology. Its boundaries have been corrected without changing its questions or point values. After correcting the worked example's expected-income answer to **Remain the same (3)**, this option records `58 / Moderately Aggressive`.

## Verified calculation under the previous form

The previous form calculated the anonymised example as follows:

| Factor | Selected answer | Score |
|---|---|---:|
| Time horizon | 8-10 years | 4 |
| Retirement proximity | Already retired | 1 |
| Regular income | Yes | 1 |
| Anticipated withdrawals | Less than 25% | 4 |
| Dependants | None | 5 |
| Age | 51-65 | 2 |
| Expected income change | Increase | 5 |
| Emergency funds | Adequate | 15 |
| Major financial obligations | None | 5 |
| Attitude to risk | Growth-oriented balanced approach | 9 |
| Tolerance for volatility | Accepts volatility over approximately three years | 9 |
| **Total** |  | **60** |

The total is therefore arithmetically correct:

`4 + 1 + 1 + 4 + 5 + 2 + 5 + 15 + 5 + 9 + 9 = 60`

The concerns below relate to what that total means and whether the methodology produces a sufficiently reliable and controlled suitability conclusion.

The `60` total accurately reproduces the selected answers. It is not the final corrected score because **Increase (5)** was selected without evidence of increasing salary, pension, rental, business or other external income. Replacing it with **Remain the same (3)** produces `58 / Moderately Aggressive`.

## 1. Overlapping risk bands

The previous form used these bands:

| Score | Risk category |
|---:|---|
| 11-20 | Very Conservative |
| 20-40 | Conservative |
| 40-55 | Moderate |
| 55-75 | Moderately Aggressive |
| 75-85 | Aggressive |

Scores `20`, `40`, `55` and `75` each fall into two categories. A score of `55`, for example, could be classified as either Moderate or Moderately Aggressive.

A controlled methodology must use unambiguous boundaries. If these categories were retained, the bands would need to be expressed without overlap, such as `11-20`, `21-40`, `41-55`, `56-75` and `76-85`.

## 2. Disproportionate factor weighting

The emergency-funds question awarded:

| Answer | Score |
|---|---:|
| No emergency savings | 1 |
| Small amount of savings | 6 |
| Adequate savings | 15 |

This creates a 14-point difference from one question. By comparison:

- time horizon ranges from `1` to `5`;
- retirement proximity ranges from `1` to `5`;
- regular-income need ranges from `1` to `5`;
- dependants range from `1` to `5`;
- age ranges from `1` to `5`; and
- expected income change ranges from `1` to `5`.

An adequate-emergency-funds answer can therefore outweigh several indicators of reduced risk capacity. The methodology does not explain or evidence why emergency liquidity should carry substantially more weight than retirement status, regular-income dependence or time horizon.

## 3. The result can overstate a retiree's risk capacity

In the anonymised example, the relevant facts include:

- the client is already retired;
- regular investment income is required;
- the aggregate portfolio draw is material;
- one account has a high draw relative to its reviewed value; and
- the voluntary-account withdrawal is high relative to that account's reviewed value.

Those facts indicate material income dependency, capital-depletion risk and sequencing risk. Nevertheless, the `15` emergency-funds points, together with `5` points for no dependants and `5` points for no major obligations, materially increased the total and helped produce a Moderately Aggressive classification.

This does not prove that the client cannot hold growth assets. It shows that the total score may overstate financial risk capacity unless the adviser separately considers retirement-income sustainability and account-level withdrawals.

## 4. The expected-income answer was incorrectly completed

The question is valid. It asks whether the client's salary, pension, rental, business or other external income is expected to decrease, remain the same or increase during the investment period. An expected increase in reliable external income may support greater financial risk capacity.

In the worked example, **Increase (5)** appears to have been selected because an increase in portfolio withdrawals was being considered. A withdrawal from invested capital is not an increase in external income. Increasing that withdrawal can reduce future financial flexibility and increase depletion risk.

The completion guidance should distinguish between:

- external earned or guaranteed income expected to increase;
- inflation-linked pension or annuity income;
- discretionary withdrawals funded from invested capital; and
- uncertain or declining income.

For the worked example, the supported answer is **Remain the same (3)**. The proposed portfolio-income change is addressed separately under anticipated withdrawals and income sustainability.

## 5. No explicitly scored investment-objective factor

The previous form scored major financial obligations but did not expressly score the primary investment objective. It did not directly distinguish among objectives such as:

- short-term capital preservation;
- sustainable income with capital preservation;
- income and capital growth;
- greater capital growth; or
- maximum long-term growth.

The investment objective is central to product and portfolio suitability. KCAS includes it explicitly so that a score cannot be interpreted without identifying what the investment must achieve.

## 6. Capacity and tolerance were blended into one total

Risk tolerance and risk capacity are related but different:

- **Risk tolerance** concerns the client's willingness and emotional ability to accept volatility and loss.
- **Risk capacity** concerns the client's financial ability to absorb loss without undermining income, liquidity or essential objectives.

A client can be comfortable with volatility while lacking the financial capacity to tolerate prolonged losses because regular withdrawals are required. In a simple additive model, high tolerance scores can compensate mathematically for low capacity scores and conceal this contradiction.

The adviser should therefore consider the dimensions separately before reaching the final profile. The total should inform, but not replace, the suitability analysis.

## 7. The indicated allocation did not align with the recorded portfolio

The previous form classified the worked-example score as Moderately Aggressive. In the source case, the recorded allocations fell outside the corresponding indicative ranges for multiple asset classes.

This does not automatically require a switch, because classification, tax, liquidity, product restrictions and the client's objectives must also be considered. It does require the adviser to document one of two conclusions:

1. how the portfolio will be adjusted to improve suitability; or
2. why the existing allocation remains suitable, including any informed client departure and warnings.

The previous document identified the profile and ranges but did not adequately force this contradiction to be resolved before issue.

## 8. The allocation ranges were only loosely constrained

The printed percentage ranges did not establish a unique or necessarily coherent portfolio. Depending on which values were selected within each range, the combined allocation could total materially less or more than 100%.

Indicative ranges can be useful, but the methodology should explain:

- whether the ranges are hard limits or guidance;
- how overlapping asset or fund classifications are treated;
- how offshore exposure interacts with asset-class exposure;
- what degree of deviation requires an adviser explanation; and
- how the final recommended allocation is tested to total 100%.

## 9. Methodology governance was unclear

The previous PDF did not clearly identify:

- a methodology version;
- an effective date;
- the approving Key Individual or governance authority;
- the approval date;
- a change history;
- the rationale for the factor weights; or
- why its questions, scoring and labels differed from previous Kanaan advice records.

Without those controls, staff and reviewers cannot determine whether the form is an approved replacement methodology, a working draft or an edited operational template.

## 10. The score was treated too readily as the conclusion

A risk score is one input into advice. It does not by itself establish that a portfolio or income strategy is suitable.

The final advice decision must also consider:

- the client's needs and objectives;
- financial position and retirement status;
- income and liquidity requirements;
- withdrawal sustainability;
- investment horizon;
- knowledge and experience;
- existing products and restrictions;
- costs, fees and tax;
- concentration and diversification;
- guarantees and capital risk; and
- any information declined or assumptions made.

KCAS records these matters alongside the score and requires a recommendation rationale, material fact sources, products considered, review findings and independent approval.

## What the evidenced KCAS methodology would produce

KCAS also retains an evidenced Kanaan advice methodology derived from retained signed advice records. Mapping the anonymised worked-example answers into that method gives:

| KCAS factor | Score |
|---|---:|
| Time horizon | 8 |
| Retirement proximity | 1 |
| Regular income | 2 |
| Anticipated withdrawal | 4 |
| Dependants | 4 |
| Age | 2 |
| Expected income change | 2 |
| Emergency funds | 5 |
| Investment objective | 9 |
| Attitude to risk | 9 |
| Volatility tolerance | 9 |
| **Total** | **55** |

Under the evidenced KCAS bands, `55` is **Medium**.

This does not mean that the previous PDF's result of `60` was added incorrectly. It correctly totals the answers that were selected, but the expected-income answer was not supported. With that answer corrected, the selectable previous-form methodology produces `58 / Moderately Aggressive`, while the evidenced KCAS methodology would produce `55 / Medium`. They remain different methodologies and should not be presented as interchangeable.

## Current operating decision

Kanaan has elected to retain both methodologies as explicit choices. Accordingly:

1. Every advice case must record which methodology was selected.
2. The evidenced KCAS methodology remains available and produces `55 / Medium` for the corrected mapped worked example.
3. The previous-form methodology is also available with half-open, non-overlapping bands and produces `58 / Moderately Aggressive` for the same worked example.
4. A case using the previous-form methodology must identify that selection explicitly in its controlled record.
5. The final Risk Analyser and CAR must be generated from the KCAS structured case and identify the selected methodology.
6. The adviser must still explain whether the existing portfolio and income strategy are suitable; selecting the previous form does not remove the weighting and capacity concerns documented above.
7. Any future methodology change must be versioned, approved, dated, tested at every boundary and implemented consistently in KCAS before use.

## Minimum requirements for a future methodology change

Before Kanaan adopts a revised point system, it should:

1. define non-overlapping score bands;
2. document the rationale for every factor and weight;
3. separate or explicitly evaluate risk capacity, risk tolerance, objective and liquidity;
4. distinguish external income growth from increased capital withdrawals;
5. define how contradictions and mandatory risk constraints affect the final result;
6. define coherent indicative asset-allocation rules;
7. require reasons for adviser overrides and material allocation deviations;
8. obtain formal governance or Key Individual approval;
9. record the version and effective date;
10. test all boundaries and representative client scenarios; and
11. implement the approved methodology identically in KCAS and every generated client document.
